Facing and lag

How this works

Banjo starts standing still. The move is played once on a flat test floor to find the update where his feet come down through the floor, and the exact distance he moves on each update. Those steps are then walked backwards from the crack, float by float, to the exact standing spot that lands him on it. Each setup is then played in this room with every lag pattern. One long update means one update of 3 or more frames; every length from 3 frames up has the same effect, because the game caps the time step. The window covers 40 updates (80 frames). A start spot next to a wall can't be stood on, because the wall pushes Banjo off it; those are counted and skipped.

Search type

Run-up searches run with the stick in straight lines, then do the final move onto the crack. Chain of moves builds the route from whole moves (punches, punch cancels, crouch turns, first-person twirls, talon trot, walks, pauses…) with Banjo standing still between them, like the 810 routes. Exact (810 method) is the old 810 GPU search: crouch-face turns from the camera, an exact table back from the crack, and every move sequence forward from the start.

Chain moves

A chain route is a list of whole moves from the start, each played to the end with Banjo standing still after it (like the 810 punch / punch-cancel / crouch-turn routes), then the final move. Pick the moves the search may use; every route is played in the exact engine, so what it finds replays exactly.

Exact search (810 method)

The search that found the 810 routes (s34), running in this page. Every move is an exact flat-floor model checked against the engine: punch, punch cancel after 1 update, punch + Z, barges. Turns are crouch-faces (crouch, stick in one of 8 directions), whose facing comes from the camera, plus first-person twirls. It builds an exact table of every spot a few moves back from the crack, then tries every move sequence from the start until one lands in the table. It runs on your CPU cores (this computer: ) and, if you turn it on, your graphics card (). Every route the graphics card finds is checked again on the CPU. The last move is always a 1-update punch cancel onto the crack. Every route found is replayed in the full engine before it is shown. PAL only.

Settings

How the search works

A crack is a gap in the floor only about 0.00002 units wide, and it is only open at some points along the seam. A route has to land Banjo's foot on one of those exact positions, at the update he comes down through the floor. So the search is brute force, like a TAS bot.

Deep mode first runs straight at every stick angle toward the target, then keeps trying run-ups of two or three legs (random first legs, last leg at the target). After every update it jumps with every A-hold length and checks each landing exactly. Landings that hit a gap are replayed from the start in the full engine. The status line shows the closest landing so far next to the gap width. Most cracks need tens of millions of paths, so let it run for minutes. All-moves mode tries every finishing move, but checks far fewer paths.

Platforms, the warp cauldron, switches and other moving or animated objects are not in the room data, because their collision changes every frame. This room

Collision from the PAL ROM: the room model plus static objects (note doors, props, breakables in their fresh-save pose). Moving and animated objects are left out.

What is exact

Collision is a bit-exact port of the game's resolver, including static objects moved into model space with the game's own matrices. Movement is ported from the decomp and matched update for update against BizHawk (PAL): walking, punch, punch cancel at 11 A timings, crouch turns, 11 jump heights, 6 backflips and 3 beak busters. Water, flight, transformations and moving enemies are not modelled. The camera yaw is held fixed.

Timing

An update lasts frames × 1/50 s on PAL (cap 0.06 s) and frames × 1/60 s on NTSC (cap 0.05 s). Two frames per update is normal; three or more is lag.
